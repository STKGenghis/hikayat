# Learning model

## Learning hypothesis

People may understand AI harms more deeply when they encounter the evidence and
pressures preceding the harm, commit to an intervention, and then compare their
reasoning with consequences and perspectives revealed afterward.

Hikayat therefore uses a cycle of situated judgment and reflective debrief:

**experience → decision → consequence → explanation → reconsideration**

The simulation creates productive uncertainty; the debrief converts that
experience into explicit learning.

## What the player practises

Hikayat prioritises five capabilities:

1. **Signal recognition** — noticing anomalies, affected voices, missing data,
   and incentives that deserve scrutiny.
2. **Stakeholder reasoning** — considering people beyond the immediate user,
   customer, operator, or institution.
3. **Systems reasoning** — tracing feedback loops and second-order effects
   rather than treating harm as a single model error.
4. **Intervention judgment** — comparing changes to the model, product,
   process, governance, and remedy.
5. **Epistemic humility** — distinguishing evidence, assumption, uncertainty,
   and hindsight.

## Scenario learning arc

Each simulation should define:

- one primary learning question;
- two or three supporting concepts;
- the weak signals available before each decision;
- stakeholder perspectives that challenge the obvious interpretation;
- plausible interventions at different system layers;
- immediate, delayed, and transferred consequences; and
- counterfactual questions for replay or discussion.

For **AI Companion for Social Anxiety**, the primary question could be:

> When a system provides genuine short-term comfort, what evidence justifies
> intervening in an engagement pattern that may be weakening a user's agency?

Supporting concepts might include proxy objectives, emotional dependency,
choice architecture, duty of care, and human escalation.

## Debrief structure

The debrief is part of the experience, not an answer screen. It should contain:

1. **Route reconstruction** — evidence inspected and actions taken.
2. **Consequence profile** — who benefited, who carried risk, and what remained
   unresolved across the six dimensions.
3. **Missed and ambiguous signals** — presented without shaming the player.
4. **Concept connection** — concise explanation of relevant AI-harm and
   governance ideas.
5. **Counterfactual comparison** — alternative interventions and the trade-offs
   they would have introduced.
6. **Reflection** — one question about reasoning and one about application
   beyond the simulation.

Avoid “correct/incorrect” labels unless a decision violates an explicit factual
or procedural constraint. Prefer language such as “this reduced immediate
exposure but transferred risk to…”

## Assessment approach

The original corpus's MCQs, scenario questions, and reflection prompts remain
valuable source material, but they should not interrupt the simulation.

For the first release, evaluation should focus on evidence of learning rather
than a player score:

- pre/post identification of the central harm;
- ability to name affected stakeholders;
- quality of the player's stated decision rationale;
- recognition of a second-order consequence;
- change in reasoning after route comparison; and
- qualitative feedback on agency, clarity, and emotional engagement.

No claim of educational effectiveness should be made from completion rates or a
small convenience sample. A later study would require a protocol, consent,
appropriate data governance, and analysis planned before collection.

## Authoring safeguards

- Separate evidence from the author's interpretation in scenario data.
- Make value judgments visible in the debrief rather than embedding them as an
  unexplained score.
- Include affected stakeholders as agents, not merely consequences.
- Avoid implying that technical safeguards alone resolve institutional harm.
- Review sensitive scenarios for stereotyping, trauma, and inadvertent
  normalisation of abuse.
- Cite external factual claims introduced during adaptation.
